A lower third is the name strap shown near the bottom of the screen. This guide builds one from
Studio's ready-made recipe, so you start with a working, editable graphic rather than a blank
canvas.

## 1. Add the recipe

Above the canvas, open **+ Recipe…** and choose **Lower Third**. Studio adds a group of ordinary
layers with editable data fields and an entrance and exit animation already in place.

The same menu offers **Bug / DOG**, **Ticker**, **Scoreboard**, **Clock / Timer** and **Countdown**.
Each recipe creates normal layers, so everything stays editable afterwards.

## 2. Edit the design

- Select a text layer on the canvas or in **Layers**, then change its content, font and colour in
  **Properties**.
- Select the panel shapes to change their fill, size or position.
- Use the arrange and align buttons above the canvas to tidy the layout.

If your project has a Brand Kit, use its colours and fonts instead of typing values, so the lower
third matches the rest of your package.

## 3. Check the animation

Press **Space** with the timeline focused to play. It plays straight through; turn on **Stop at
Steps** to make it wait at each OGraf step, the moment where the graphic waits on air for the
operator. See [Animate with steps and keyframes](/guides/animate) to change the timing.

## 4. Preview it as an operator would

Open **Preview & Export**:

- **Next step ⏭** takes the graphic in, or on to its next step.
- **Take Out** plays the exit.
- **Fullscreen** fills the display with the graphic. In fullscreen, **Space** or **Right Arrow**
  goes to the next step, **Left Arrow** to the previous one, and **Escape** exits.

## 5. Save the project

Choose **File → Save** and pick a location. Studio writes `Template Name.ogs`, the editable source,
and later saves go to the same file. **Save As…** saves it under a new name.

To pick the frame used for the thumbnail, choose **File → Save Template Copy…**, set the frame, then
choose **Save .ogs…**. **Download ZIP** there gives you the `.ogs` together with a transparent
thumbnail PNG.

Keep the `.ogs` file: it is what you reopen to make changes. When the graphic is ready for playout,
[export an OGraf package](/guides/export).
