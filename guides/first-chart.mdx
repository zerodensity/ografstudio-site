A chart is an ordinary layer drawn by the Chart.js plugin, so it stays editable: its values are rows
in **Properties**, its type is a tile you click, and its motion is a few timing fields. This guide
builds a four-value bar chart from a blank template, then shows what changes for the other eight
chart types and how to feed a chart from a file.

It follows Studio 0.30 at 1920 × 1080 and 50 fps. The values are fictional team data, so your
canvas matches the screenshots. Replace them with your own afterwards.

## 1. Start a blank template

1. Save anything you have open with **File → Save Template**.
2. Choose **File → New…**, pick **1920 × 1080** and **50 fps**, and click **Blank template**.
3. Pick **Essentials** from the **Layout** selector at the top: Layers on the left, the canvas in
   the centre, Properties on the right and the Timeline below.
4. Click the template name at the top and call it **My First Bar Chart**.

![File → New: choose the canvas size and frame rate, then Blank template](/img/guides/charts/new-document.webp)

The checkerboard behind the canvas is an editor guide and is never exported. For a full-screen
chart, give the composition an opaque background in **Properties** with nothing selected. For an
overlay, keep it transparent and test on real video.

## 2. Add a chart

1. Choose **Window → Plugins** and open the **Chart** category.
2. Click **Bar**. Studio adds a Chart layer to the composition. Close the Plugins panel.
3. Select the layer. **Properties** now has a **Chart** section with the same gallery of types.

![Window → Plugins → Chart adds a chart. The gallery in Properties changes the one you have.](/img/guides/charts/plugins-chart-gallery.webp)

A tile in the Plugins panel adds a new chart. The same tile in **Properties** changes the selected
one, so switch type there rather than stacking layers. If no provider is listed, check
**File → Preferences → OGraf Studio → Plugins**.

## 3. Enter the values

A chart takes its values from rows in **Properties**. No JSON is needed.

| Category | Value |
| -------- | ----- |
| FALCONS  | 72    |
| COMETS   | 58    |
| WAVES    | 46    |
| WOLVES   | 33    |

1. In **Properties → Chart**, set **Data mode** to **Independent metrics**.
2. Under **Data series**, keep one series. Use **+ Add row** until there are four rows, and **×**
   to remove any extra.
3. Type each label and value. Press **Tab** after each edit to commit it.
4. Click a swatch to give each category its colour.

![The rows editor takes names, values and colours directly](/img/guides/charts/data-rows.webp)

The **+** beside **Data series** adds another dataset, not another category. Use it for several
lines, or for the parts of a stacked bar.

**Independent metrics** keeps raw values. **Counts → share (%)** turns non-negative counts into
shares and needs a positive total. **Percentage distribution** expects values that already add up
to 100.

## 4. Size the plot

1. In the layer's **Transform**, set X 300, Y 280, Width 1000 and Height 550. That leaves room for
   a title and a legend; adjust it to your layout.
2. Keep **Auto-keyframe** off while you lay the chart out.
3. Expand **Presentation & updates**. Set the axis to a minimum of 0, a maximum of 100 and a step
   of 20, and **Maximum bar thickness** to 160.
4. Turn on **Value labels**, with the precision and suffix your units need.
5. Set **Data update** to **Animate changed values**, so a new number moves the bar instead of
   redrawing it.

![Presentation & updates: axis range, type, bar thickness, value labels and update mode](/img/guides/charts/presentation-updates.webp)

Readability comes before filling the frame. If four bars look lost, narrow the plot instead of
spreading them out. Maximum bar thickness is a cap, not a gap setting.

## 5. Animate the bars

A chart has three places its motion can come from. Knowing which one you are editing stops the
whole chart from sliding when you meant to grow its bars.

| Control                            | What it moves                                             | Use it for                                                |
| ---------------------------------- | --------------------------------------------------------- | --------------------------------------------------------- |
| **Chart → Animation**              | The marks, with the renderer's own Grow, Reveal and Fade  | Bars growing from the baseline, a line tracing left to right |
| **Chart → Bars / slices animation** | Individual bars or slices, with a plugin preset           | A separate In or Out for each mark                        |
| **Animate In / Out**               | The whole chart layer                                     | A deliberate exit or framing move for the entire plot     |

1. In the **Timeline**, set **Length → In** to **2s** and **Out** to **1s**: 100 frames in and 50
   out at 50 fps.
2. Set **Chart animation** to **Grow**, with a duration of 0.72 s, a delay of 0.24 s and a stagger
   of 0.24 s. Four bars finish at 1.68 s, inside the 2-second entrance.
3. Leave **Bars / slices In** on **Use chart animation**. A mark preset replaces the native
   entrance; the two do not play together.
4. For the exit, set **Animate Out** to **Wipe**, **To bottom**, 1 s. It wipes the whole plot away,
   axes included.
5. Scrub early, middle and settled frames, then play it with **Take In** and **Take Out** in
   **Preview & Export**.

![Chart animation: Grow, with its duration, delay and stagger](/img/guides/charts/chart-animation-grow.webp)

**Bars / slices animation** works on bars, stacked bars and the sectors of pie, doughnut and
polar-area charts, with presets such as Slide & Settle, Pulse Pop and Curtain Reveal. Pick one,
adjust it and click **Apply**. Lines, areas and radars are left out.

[Animate with steps and keyframes](/guides/animate) covers the timeline itself.

## 6. Preview and save

1. Open **Window → Preview & Export**. Click **Take In**, change a value, and click **Take Out**.
   **Fullscreen** shows the chart at the output ratio.
2. Click **Run broadcast QA** for layout and text checks, choose the export profile and click
   **Run compatibility test**.
3. Choose **File → Save Template**, name it **My First Bar Chart**, pick a thumbnail frame with the
   chart fully visible, and click **Save .ogs…**.

![Save Template keeps the editable .ogs source](/img/guides/charts/save-template.webp)

Keep the `.ogs`: it is what you reopen to make changes. [Export an OGraf package](/guides/export)
when the chart is ready for playout, and test it in the renderer that will play it.

## Customise the prepared chart set

The [Chart set example](/examples/charts) is the same four teams across all nine types, styled and
animated, with the values as operator controls. Play it there, or download its source from that
page and open it in Studio to use its motion with your own numbers.

1. Choose **File → Open…** and open `charts.ogs`. In the **Templates** tab at the top left, select
   **02 - Bar**.
2. Open **Window → Preview & Export** and click **Take In**.
3. Click the **FALCONS** field, replace 72 with **80** and press **Tab**. The bar and its readout
   change; the other three stay. **Take Out**, then **Take In** replays the entrance.
4. Select **06 - Pie**, click **Take Out**, then **Take In**: a clockwise sweep from 12 o'clock,
   with its own exit. **07 - Doughnut** and **09 - Polar Area** work the same way.
5. Choose **File → Save Template Set…** and save **My Chart Set.ogs**. **Save Template** would
   keep only one template.

![The prepared set in fullscreen preview, with its four numeric controls](/img/guides/charts/chart-set-controls.webp)

The circular templates are masks, chart copies and bindings working together. Change the exposed
numbers, not the layers. If you do change them, reopen the original and start again.

To make new values the saved defaults for every template, open **Scene Outliner → Shared controls
and asset slots**, expand **FALCONS** and edit its shared value. Keep the template fields on
**Inherit**, then save the set again.

![Shared controls are separate from the preview's test values](/img/guides/charts/shared-controls.webp)

## Update a chart from a data file

A chart's data can also arrive as one JSON field. This exercise uses a practice template with the
binding and connection already in place.

1. Download [chart-stacked-practice.ogs](pathname:///downloads/chart-stacked-practice.ogs) and
   [chart-update-values.json](pathname:///downloads/chart-update-values.json).
2. Open the template with **File → Open…**. Choose **Window → Data → Connections** and select
   **Data connection 1**: format JSON, source Imported / pasted. **Take In** shows a stack of 80,
   65, 40 and 20.
3. Turn the connection's **Enabled** off while you edit. Click **Import CSV / JSON…** and choose
   the downloaded JSON file.
4. Keep the **Chart data (JSON)** field mapped to `chart_data` and the update target on
   **Editor preview**. Press **Tab**, enable the connection and click **Refresh now**.
5. The stack now reads FALCONS 50, COMETS 60, WAVES 40 and WOLVES 20, a total of 170, and COMETS
   is the largest segment. Save it as **My Imported Chart.ogs**.

![An Imported / pasted connection, with chart_data mapped](/img/guides/charts/imported-connection.webp)

The file is the chart's data as Chart.js expects it, inside one `chart_data` string:

```json
{
  "chart_data": "{\"labels\":[\"TEAM TOTAL\"],\"datasets\":[{\"label\":\"FALCONS\",\"data\":[50],\"backgroundColor\":\"#2563eb\",\"borderColor\":\"#2563eb\"},{\"label\":\"COMETS\",\"data\":[60],\"backgroundColor\":\"#f472b6\",\"borderColor\":\"#f472b6\"},{\"label\":\"WAVES\",\"data\":[40],\"backgroundColor\":\"#34d399\",\"borderColor\":\"#34d399\"},{\"label\":\"WOLVES\",\"data\":[20],\"backgroundColor\":\"#fbbf24\",\"borderColor\":\"#fbbf24\"}]}"
}
```

For your own data, keep the same field and mapping, and supply matching labels, finite numbers and
valid colours. A URL that returns the same payload works through the connection's **URL** source:
enter it, choose **Editor preview** and click **Refresh now** before turning on a periodic refresh.
On air, an exported graphic receives its data from the playout controller instead; see
[Connect graphics to operator data](/guides/bind-data).

## Check before you ship

- Bars are grouped with deliberate width and gaps, not stretched to fill the frame.
- Values, category labels and legends are readable and not hidden behind marks.
- Circular plots stay circular, and it is clear whether values are raw or percentages.
- In, on-air updates and Out are tested separately: bars grow in order, lines trace in order, and
  sweeps start at 12 o'clock.
- Zero, maximum, equal and long-label values are checked.
- The editable `.ogs` is saved and reopened, alongside any exported package.

The [charts reference](/docs/user-guide/layers/charts) lists every chart setting.
