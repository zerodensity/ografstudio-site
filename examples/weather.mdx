The weather slot of a news programme, playing today's real forecast. **It's live:** your browser asked
Open-Meteo for the twelve cities' weather when this page opened, and asks again every quarter of an
hour. The skies and temperatures you see are the ones forecast right now, and the line under the
player says when they came.

The presenter stands on the left and steps through the show: the map with today's highs, tomorrow's,
the day after's, and five days for one city. Three buttons on the map, **Today**, **Tomorrow** and the
day after by its name, let the presenter tap straight to any of its days. Rain, drizzle, thunder, snow
and fog fall only where they're forecast, drawn by a shader you can see the map through.

The left third stays clear for the presenter. Here it's see-through, so choose **Webcam** under the
player and stand there yourself.

## Try it

The player runs the show by itself as the page opens, round and round: the map's three days, the
outlook, a tour of the cities, and out. Press any button or the picture and it stops there, and the
controls are yours. **Autoplay** starts it again from the top.

To step through it yourself, press **Replay**, then:

1. Press **Next step**. Each city gets its sky and the day's high, west to east, and the icons move:
   rain falls, clouds drift, the sun turns.
2. Tap **Tomorrow** on the graphic, or the day after it. The numbers roll and the skies change, west to
   east, and the rain and snow move to where they'll fall that day. **Next step** goes through the same
   days in order.
3. Press **Next step** until five days in one city show. The map pushes in to it.
4. Pick another **Outlook city**. The rain, snow or fog there clears and the five days dip, then the map
   glides over to the new city in one move, a little longer for a longer trip. When it lands, the new
   city's numbers come back up and its weather comes in.
5. Tick **Tour cities**. The outlook moves on to the next city every eight seconds or so, round the
   map. The city menu waits until you untick it.
6. Press **Take out** at any step. Everything plays off together.

## What you get

- **Live data:** the page you're on is the controller. It asks Open-Meteo for the twelve cities'
  forecast and sends it to the graphic as one update, then again every quarter of an hour.
- **Steps:** Open, Today, Tomorrow, Day after and Outlook. Take out works from any of them.
- **Touch buttons:** the map's three days are all in place at once, and rules show the one picked. A
  tap and a step both just name the day, so they always agree, and the buttons do nothing once the
  outlook is up. The third button takes its weekday from the forecast.
- **A see-through shader:** one layer draws the weather over the whole map. The forecast tells it what
  falls where, and it stays off the presenter's side.
- **Functions:** one function, Weather, turns the forecast into every icon, number, colour and date on
  screen, and into the shader's weather. It also keeps time: it wakes itself when the camera lands to
  change the outlook's numbers, and to move a tour on to the next city.
- **Rules:** a new outlook city clears the weather, dips the numbers and then moves the camera, all
  inside the graphic, so any controller that changes `featured`, and the tour, gets the same move. There's one for each pair of
  cities, so a longer trip can take longer.
- **Moving icons:** each icon is a small picture that moves by itself, so the graphic draws nothing
  more for it. The function only names the sky, and the icon layer finds its picture.
- **Data updates:** a new forecast changes the numbers and icons in place, without
  starting the show again.
- **A saved sample:** the template holds a real forecast from the day it was built, so it never starts
  empty.

The [weather strap](/examples/weather-strap) plays the same forecast in a news bulletin.

## How the data gets there

A graphic doesn't fetch anything itself. Whoever plays it sends it data, so any controller that sends
these fields can drive the same package:

| Field | What it holds |
| --- | --- |
| `cities` | One entry per city: the temperature now, how it feels, the wind and humidity, tonight's low and sky, and five days of highs, lows, sky and chance of rain. Skies are WMO weather codes, as most forecast services send them |
| `featured` | The city of the five-day outlook |
| `tour` | Whether the outlook visits every city in turn. While it's on, the graphic changes `featured` itself |
| `credit` | Where the forecast comes from, at the bottom right |

Each city keeps its place on the map by its name, so the order of `cities` doesn't matter. The forecast
comes from [Open-Meteo](https://open-meteo.com/), and your browser asks for it directly while the
graphic is on screen.

## Make it yours

- Send your own forecast in `cities`. Any service with WMO codes fits as it is.
- Change the temperature colours in `chipOf` at the top of the Weather function.
- Change how hard it rains or snows in the shader's `amounts`, and its colours at the end of
  `mainImage`.
