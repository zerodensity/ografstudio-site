A markets screen for a business channel, playing live. Eight crypto markets stream in from Coinbase
Exchange while you watch, because crypto trades around the clock and its public feed needs no key.
Prices light up as they change, trades slide into the list, and the chart's last point moves with
the market. The dots beat as data arrives, so if the feed ever stops, you can see it.

For illustration only. This isn't investment advice.

## Try it

1. Click a market in the watchlist. The chart reshapes into it.
2. Change **1H**, **24H** or **7D**, and switch between **Area** and **Candles**.
3. Move your pointer over the chart to read any price and time.
4. Click **Auto tour** to let it move through the markets by itself, as it would on air. Picking a
   market turns the tour off.
5. Press **Fullscreen**. On a phone it turns to landscape however you hold it. On a computer, try the
   keyboard: the up and down arrows change market, 1, 2 and 3 change range, C switches the chart and
   T the tour.

## What you get

- **Live data:** the page you're on is the controller. It listens to the feed and sends the graphic
  ordinary data updates, twice a second. Any controller could send the same fields.
- **Functions:** one function, Markets, turns those updates into everything on screen, and another
  follows the pointer for the crosshair. Clicks stay inside the graphic, with no page code involved.
- **A chart drawn by a function:** the function writes the chart as a picture to a field, and an image
  layer shows it. That's one layer for the area, the candles and the volume bars, in any style you
  can draw.
- **Rules:** the hover highlights, clicks and keyboard shortcuts.
- **Runtime collection:** the trade list is one row designed once. New trades push the rows down.
- **Actions:** the buttons under the graphic send the same commands a controller can send.
- **A saved sample:** the template holds real prices from the day it was built, so it never starts
  empty. The status at the top says **SAMPLE DATA** until a feed arrives.

## How the data gets there

A graphic doesn't fetch anything itself. Whoever plays it sends it data, so any controller that sends
these fields can drive the same package. Opened in Studio, it plays its saved sample until it gets
some:

| Field | What it holds |
| --- | --- |
| `markets` | Up to eight markets, each with its price and its 24-hour open, high, low and volume |
| `history` | Candles for every market and range, as JSON |
| `trades` | The latest trades, newest first |
| `fx` | Exchange rates for the crawl along the bottom. Each one's `previous` rate decides its up or down arrow |
| `source` | Who the prices come from, named at the top of the graphic |
| `feed_status` | `live`, `connecting` or `reconnecting` |
| `as_of` | The time of the latest trade |

A graphic can't report your clicks to whoever plays it, so the feed sends every market and range, and
the graphic chooses what to show.

Prices come from [Coinbase Exchange](https://docs.cdp.coinbase.com/exchange/docs/welcome) and exchange
rates from the European Central Bank through [Frankfurter](https://frankfurter.dev). Those rates change
once a working day, so their arrows show the change from the day before. Your browser connects to both
directly while the graphic is on screen.

## Make it yours

- Send your own `markets`, such as stocks from your data provider, and name it in `source`. The rows
  follow your order.
- Change the colours and the pace in the numbers at the top of the Markets function.
- Change the chart's look where the function draws it, in `drawChart`.
